# Planet surface map credits

Every map here is a US government work (NASA or USGS) and is in the public domain. Each one was downloaded from the source below, resized to 2048×1024 and saved as a JPEG; any other change is listed with the file. All maps are equirectangular (simple cylindrical): longitude runs from -180° on the left edge to +180° on the right, the prime meridian is the vertical center line, and north is up.

## earth-day.jpg

- **Title:** Blue Marble: Next Generation with Topography and Bathymetry, July 2004 (`world.topo.bathy.200407.3x5400x2700.jpg`)
- **Creator:** NASA Earth Observatory / NASA Goddard Space Flight Center (Reto Stöckli). Terra MODIS land surface, with topography and bathymetry (GEBCO) shading
- **Source:** https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-topography-bathymetry/ (file: https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/eo/images/bmng/bmng-topography-bathymetry/july/world.topo.bathy.200407.3x5400x2700.jpg)
- **License:** Public domain (NASA work)
- **Changes:** Resized from 5400×2700 to 2048×1024; JPEG quality 82.

## earth-clouds.jpg

- **Title:** The Blue Marble (2002): Clouds (`cloud_combined_2048.jpg`)
- **Creator:** NASA Goddard Space Flight Center. Image by Reto Stöckli (land surface, shallow water, clouds), enhancements by Robert Simmon. Terra MODIS: two days of visible imagery plus a third day of thermal infrared over the poles
- **Source:** https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-blue-marble-true-color-global-imagery-at-1km-resolution/ (file: https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/eo/content-feature/bluemarble/images/cloud_combined_2048.jpg)
- **License:** Public domain (NASA work)
- **Changes:** Already 2048×1024; saved as a grayscale JPEG at quality 70.

## mercury.jpg

- **Title:** Mercury MESSENGER MDIS Basemap MD3 Color Global Mosaic 665m (`Mercury_MESSENGER_MDIS_Basemap_MD3Color_Mosaic_Global_665m.tif`)
- **Creator:** NASA / Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory / Carnegie Institution of Washington (MESSENGER MDIS); mosaic by Applied Coherent Technology Corporation; published by USGS Astrogeology Science Center. The color comes from the 1000 nm, 750 nm and 430 nm filters in red, green and blue, so it's stronger than what the eye would see
- **Source:** https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/mercury_messenger_mdis_basemap_md3_color_global_mosaic_665m (file: https://planetarymaps.usgs.gov/mosaic/Mercury_MESSENGER_MDIS_Basemap_MD3Color_Mosaic_Global_665m.tif). Also on Wikimedia Commons, marked public domain (PD USGS): https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mercury_MESSENGER_MDIS_Basemap_MD3Color_Mosaic_Global_665m.tif
- **License:** Public domain (NASA mission data published by USGS). The USGS catalog asks users to "please cite authors", which the credit above does.
- **Changes:** Resized from 23040×11520 to 2048×1024; JPEG quality 62. The mosaic has no data near the poles (black above about 84°N and below about 83°S). Those black pixels were filled with a horizontally blurred copy of the nearest complete row, so the poles don't render as black dots.
